🔍 Browser Focus Mode Extension

A Chrome extension that flips the focus blocker model: instead of blocking what distracts you, it defines what you need. Full product arc from discovery interviews through PRD, metrics, roadmap, and Calm Technology principles.

Calm Technology System Design User Research PRD Writing

The Problem

When a user is trying to focus in a particular browser tab, the presence of other open tabs, along with the ability to open new ones, creates a constant source of distraction. The average user has 17 tabs open at any given time, and productivity communities consistently identify tab-switching as a primary focus killer.

Discovery method: Personal experience as a proxy user, validated through five structured user interviews. Core discipline: ask about the past, never the future.

Sample interview questions:

  • "Tell me about an instance where you felt distracted by multiple tabs open in your browser."
  • "Tell me about the last time you had to do focused work. What was the setup you had in place before starting?"
  • "Walk me through a time when, despite all measures you had in place, you still ended up getting distracted."

The User

Primary segment: Students and knowledge workers doing self-directed deep work without a hard external deadline enforcing focus.

Jobs To Be Done: When a student or knowledge worker is trying to focus on a task and is feeling distracted, they want to accomplish the task at hand successfully within a given time, so they can finish what they set out to do and feel their time was genuinely well spent.

Key insight: The real job is not "block distracting websites." It is "create conditions where I can do my best work and feel the satisfaction of finishing what I started."

The Market

Product Core Bet Strength Blind Spot
Cold Turkey Block what distracts you Most aggressive blocker; application-level whitelisting No browser-level tab control; cannot define an intentional workspace
Freedom Cross-platform restriction with scheduling Available on mobile; syncs across devices Same subtractive model; no tab-level workspace control
StayFocusd Lightweight browser-based time limits Free Chrome extension; simple to set up Time limits cap distraction but don't prevent it; no workspace concept
Cold Turkey blocks what you don't want. This extension defines what you do want. That is the difference between building walls and building a room.

The Solution (PRD)

A Chrome extension allowing users to initiate a timed focus session capped at 90 minutes. Once a session begins, the browser enters a locked state: the user cannot switch to any tab outside a pre-defined group of approved tabs. If a user attempts to exit before the timer completes, they see a 15-second countdown to confirm. The goal is pause, not punishment.

Non-goals for v1: No customisable UI · No session lockout · No Pomodoro timer · No cross-device sync · Chrome only

Success Metrics

Metric Definition Rationale
North Star Distinct users completing 2+ focus sessions per week Signals habit formation, not just trial
Guardrail Uninstall rate after sessions Catches long-term failure even when short-term looks healthy
Input (30 days) Users completing 15+ sessions in first month Signal the product has become routine, not novelty

Roadmap

Now: Sprint 1 Next: Sprint 2 & 3 Later: When conditions are right
Tab locking with interval sessions (max 90 min) Tab border highlighting (Calm Tech Principle 5) Motivational quotes on exit, only after user testing
Chained tab groups (approved workspace) Countdown before session starts (Calm Tech Principle 2) Session completion celebration
15-second countdown exit mechanism Peripheral time indicator (Calm Tech Principle 3) Optional to-do list, only if validated

Key Trade-offs

  • Exit friction over hard lockout: 15-second countdown trades absolute enforcement for user trust.
  • Chrome only in v1: Covers ~65% of browser market share. A polished Chrome experience beats a mediocre multi-browser one.
  • No customisation in v1: Personalising a focus app contradicts its core purpose (Calm Technology Principle 1).

📝 The Middle Ground Note-Taking App

A note-taking app for the user every major product ignores: the middle user who needs more than Apple Notes but less than Notion. Competitive analysis, user persona, PRD, and the discipline of resisting feature creep.

User Research PRD Writing Competitive Analysis Market Strategy

The Problem

The mid-20s to early 30s age group (students and working professionals) are being let down by existing note-taking apps. No app is easy enough to use immediately without configuration, yet powerful enough for both short-form capture and long-form collaboration.

Three sources of evidence:

  • Personal experience: As a postgraduate student, the friction of switching between tools and Notion's setup failure were experienced directly.
  • Competitive analysis: Every product either optimised for simplicity and became limiting, or optimised for power and became inaccessible.
  • Behavioural evidence: Users routing around Google Docs for lightweight capture (using Apple Notes or WhatsApp instead) is strong evidence of a product-job mismatch.

The User

User persona: James, 25. A master's student in the US. Collaborates with classmates on group projects and does his own writing. Finds Google Docs good for collaboration but poor for personal work.

Once, James spent 45 minutes customising a Notion template for his job search. He ended up so frustrated that he paid for an AI recruiter instead, because the AI was easier to set up than the template.

Jobs To Be Done: When I need to write, organise, or collaborate on something, I want a tool I can open and use immediately without configuration, so I can focus on the work itself rather than setting up the environment for the work.

The Market

Product Core Bet Strength Blind Spot
Google Docs Documents belong to teams Real-time co-editing, version history Architecturally heavy for solo use; users route around it for casual capture
Apple Notes Frictionless capture above all else Instantly accessible; no account needed Designed for capture, not knowledge management; breaks down at scale
Evernote One tool for everything Cross-platform, powerful search Added too many features until the core promise became diluted
Notion Infinite flexibility Database-as-document; genuinely new Blank canvas is paralysing; users spend more time building systems than doing work
If you are trying to build a product that benefits the user, this is the kind of friction you have to hold out against.

The Solution

A note-taking app enabling collaboration and long/short-form writing without configuration. Open and start writing immediately.

The differentiator: Every feature must earn its place by solving a real user problem, not by responding to competitive pressure.

Success Metrics

Metric Definition Rationale
North Star Distinct users creating 2+ docs (200+ chars) per week OR contributing to a shared doc Captures both solo and collaborative use
Guardrail 14-day inactive rate Rising rate signals onboarding is failing to deliver the frictionless promise
Input (30 days) % of new users completing first doc within 24 hours Tells us immediately if the no-configuration promise is being delivered

Roadmap

Now: Sprint 1 Next: Sprint 2 & 3 Later: When conditions are right
Core writing editor (bold, italic, headings, bullets) Templates: 2 or 3 pre-built structures AI writing assistance, when a capable open source model is ready
Real-time collaboration with link sharing How-to guide on first login Additional integrations, user feedback only, never competitive pressure
Clean minimal UI, no configuration required Opt-in weekly digest of what the user created

Key Trade-offs

  • Resisting competitive iteration: Not iterating in response to competitor releases. Slower feature velocity preserves the core promise long term.
  • Real value over vanity metrics: Documents completed and collaborative contributions over daily active users or time in app.

🎵 Spotify: Discover Music

A strategic recommendation to solve genuine music discovery for the niche listener segment. Spotify's existing infrastructure creates a structural ceiling: the more specific a user's taste, the less useful features become over time. Discover Music proposes a natural language discovery mode powered by a two-layer data architecture including a community music intelligence layer.

Competitive Analysis User Research PRD Writing Market Strategy Business Case Go-to-Market

The Problem

Spotify's recommendation engine is not broken. It is working exactly as designed. The algorithm is a feedback loop that prioritizes songs with more global consumption data, structurally favoring popular content and marginalizing niche artists. The algorithm shifted further toward familiarity in 2025 — Autoplay, the AI DJ, and Song Radio now recycle tracks users already know.

Only 27% of users agree that streaming services help them discover music outside familiar territory. 42% actively disagree. That gap represents hundreds of millions of users whose primary discovery need is unmet by everything Spotify currently offers, many of them premium subscribers.

Spotify attempted niche discovery through Discovery Mode, but that works for mid-tier artists trying to break through — not for artists whose ceiling is genuinely small. The structural problem is data architecture. Every existing feature begins from listening history and finds something adjacent. Discover Music removes that ceiling entirely.

The User

Primary persona: 25 to 35-year-old with eclectic cross-genre taste. Listens seriously. Pays for premium.

DimensionDetail
Current behaviorUses Smart Shuffle and Discover Weekly. After a few days, recommendations become cyclic. Retreats to familiar songs when discovery stops working.
Discovery loopWhen Spotify fails them, they find music outside the app through social media, friend recommendations, or YouTube. The app is where they store music after discovery has happened elsewhere.
Core frustrationInstalled a music app to expand their musical world. Using it as a jukebox for songs they already know.
Switching costYears of behavioral history trained Spotify's algorithm to their specific taste. That history does not transfer to any competing platform — arriving at YouTube Music means starting cold.

The Two Jobs:

  • Job 1 (Spotify does well): Serve me well within what I already know and love. High switching cost keeps users embedded despite discovery frustrations.
  • Job 2 (Spotify fails structurally): Take me somewhere I have never been but know I would love. This job cannot be completed with behavioral data alone — it requires a different data source.
Spotify knows what a user has heard. It cannot know what a user has not yet heard but would love. That gap cannot be closed by a better algorithm trained on the same data.

Competitive Landscape

PlatformStrengthFailure for the niche listener
Spotify 761M MAUs, unmatched behavioral data depth, strongest algorithmic discovery infrastructure, 100M+ track catalog. Recommendation engine anchored to listening history. Structurally biased toward popular content. Niche listeners hit a cyclic recommendation ceiling regardless of time on platform.
Apple Music Spatial Audio and lossless quality at no extra cost. Over 100 human editors globally curating genre-specific playlists — the deepest editorial curation in the market. Apple-only, excluding all Android users. Editorial curation is passive: the user cannot tell Apple Music what they are looking for. No intent-based discovery mechanism exists.
YouTube Music Broader content including live sessions, covers, and unofficial recordings. Leverages over a decade of YouTube watch history as behavioral signal. Switching means losing years of algorithm training no migration tool can transfer. Discovery designed for clear genre preferences, not cross-genre listeners.

Every existing discovery feature begins from what a user already listens to or relies on passive editorial decisions. None have built a mechanism where the user states their intent and the product steps entirely outside their history to fulfill it. That is the gap Discover Music fills.

The Product Recommendation

A new tile on the Spotify home screen called Discover Music. When tapped, users enter a dedicated discovery session where they describe the kind of music they want in their own words. Spotify generates a playlist drawing from the full catalogue — not just listening history — and presents it with a visual map showing how familiar or unfamiliar each track is.

Three differentiators from the AI DJ:

  • Text input over conversational persona: The DJ accepts voice commands through a persona. Discover Music accepts written descriptions, anchoring output to specific user intent rather than a DJ's interpretation.
  • Full track playback: The DJ plays partial tracks for background listening. Discover Music plays every track completely, serving the job of finding music to save and return to.
  • Intensity filter with a history-off mode: Take it easy (70% familiar, 30% new), Go crazy (30% familiar, 70% new), and Music from another planet (listening history ignored entirely). The DJ cannot offer a history-off mode by design.

The Community Intelligence Layer: Current Spotify infrastructure matches descriptions against acoustic similarity and catalogue metadata. Discover Music adds a community intelligence layer built from genre-specific listener discussion communities seeded and moderated by Spotify directly. Passionate listeners make connections between artists, sounds, moods, and eras that no acoustic algorithm can replicate. NLP extracts that knowledge and uses it as a recommendation signal.

10 launch communities seeded 2–3 months before release: Hip-Hop, Pop, Rock, Electronic/EDM, Latin, R&B/Soul, K-Pop, Afrobeats, Country, and Indie/Alternative. Minimum 500 posts per genre community before launch. Below this threshold the feature defaults to catalogue metadata.

Business Case

Discovery is already Spotify's strongest retention lever. AI-powered features drive 40% higher retention rates and 140 minutes of daily listening versus 99 minutes for non-AI users. Personalized playlists contribute to 46% of premium conversions.

The 42% of users who feel discovery features are not serving them are not being poorly served by a feature that could be tuned. They are being structurally excluded by an architecture that cannot serve them without a different data source — and they are not generating return on Spotify's existing discovery investment.

At 293 million premium subscribers paying approximately $11 per month, a 1% improvement in premium retention represents over $38 million in annualised revenue. Discover Music targets users showing playlist fatigue signals — the cohort at highest churn risk — making it a direct revenue protection play.

Go-to-Market Strategy

PhaseCohortGoal
Phase 1 (0–30 days)Playlist fatigue users — premium subscribers who have not added new music in 30+ daysQuality validation in hardest conditions. If it re-engages this cohort, the retention story is direct and commercially significant.
Phase 2 (30–90 days)Power users — premium subscribers with high Discover Weekly engagementRetention reinforcement. A/B test the Discovery Map visual. Monitor correlation with artist follows and playlist saves.
Phase 3 (90+ days)All premium users, then free tier 6 months laterFull platform scale. Free tier: 3 sessions/week, ad-supported, hard wall upgrade prompt at limit.

Risk Assessment

RiskLikelihoodMitigation
Major label conflict over stream diversion to independent artistsHigh without proactive managementNegotiate minimum stream percentage guarantee for major label content before launch. Surface through deep catalog tracks, not exclusively independent artists.
Playlist quality misses: generated playlist feels irrelevant to descriptionHigh in early iterationsClosed beta with tight feedback loop. In-session prompt: "Did you discover something you would not have found otherwise?" Use responses to calibrate before broad release.
Community intelligence layer low signal in niche genresMediumSeed 10 genre communities 2–3 months before launch with minimum 500 posts each. Layer 1 catalogue data acts as fallback when community signal is thin.

Success Metrics

Primary: Feature-attributed track saves — songs first encountered through Discover Music subsequently saved to liked songs or a personal playlist, measured against a baseline of saves from standard recommendations.

Downstream:

  • Post-discovery artist follows: users who follow a new artist after a Discover Music session
  • Session return rate: users who complete more than one session within 30 days
  • Playlist fatigue conversion rate: percentage of fatigue-cohort users who return to active music-adding behavior within 30 days of first session
  • Free-to-premium conversion rate: percentage of free users who upgrade after hitting the three-session weekly limit

Guardrails:

  • Session abandonment rate within the first 60 seconds
  • AI DJ cannibalisation rate
  • Label stream distribution: percentage of Discover Music-generated streams going to major label content, tracked against the negotiated minimum guarantee
